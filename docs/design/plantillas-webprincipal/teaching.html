Pedagogy & Curriculum

My teaching philosophy is rooted in the conviction that rigorous economic theory must be paired with empirical reality. I strive to cultivate critical thinking, equipping students with the analytical tools necessary to dissect complex societal challenges and formulate evidence-based policy solutions.

Graduate Seminars

Advanced coursework designed for Ph.D. and Master's candidates, emphasizing contemporary methodologies and theoretical frontiers.

Econ 820 Fall 2024

Advanced Behavioral Economics

Active

This seminar explores the psychological foundations of economic behavior. We critically evaluate standard neoclassical models against empirical anomalies and construct alternative theoretical frameworks incorporating bounded rationality, social preferences, and self-control problems.

Econ 845 Spring 2024

Applied Econometrics & Causal Inference

Focuses on quasi-experimental methods for estimating causal effects in observational data. Topics include instrumental variables, difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity designs, and synthetic control methods, applied to current economic policy debates.
